# The U.S. gender pay gap

Source: [Pew — U.S. gender pay gap](../sources/us-gender-pay-gap-pew.md)

## The figures — two different measures, not comparable to each other

- **85%** — women's median hourly earnings as a share of men's, full- and
  part-time workers combined, 2024.
- **82 cents to the dollar** (2022) vs. **80 cents to the dollar** (2002) —
  a different measure from Pew's 2023 report, essentially flat over two
  decades.
- **95 cents to the dollar** — women aged 25–34, 2024, a much narrower gap
  than the all-worker figure.

[Pew — U.S. gender pay gap](../sources/us-gender-pay-gap-pew.md)

## Three named causes

1. **Caregiving / time out of the workforce.** Once education is controlled
   for, the raw mother/non-mother pay gap is **"driven mainly by
   differences in educational attainment,"** not motherhood itself. What's
   real: younger mothers work **two fewer hours per week** on average, and
   fathers get a **"fatherhood wage premium"** unrelated to education.
2. **Occupational segregation.** Women are underrepresented in managerial
   and STEM roles, overrepresented in education, health care, and personal
   care/services — lower-paying fields.
3. **Discrimination / unconscious bias.** New evidence of hiring
   discrimination exists, but Pew calls these factors **"difficult to
   quantify."**

## How this relates to Korea's pay-gap source

This wiki also has a Korea pay-gap figure — **29.3%**, worst in the OECD —
from [Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md). The two are **not
directly comparable**: different countries, different measurement
methodologies (OECD full-time median-earnings gap for Korea vs. Pew's
hourly full+part-time measure for the U.S.). Korea's source also describes
a proposed transparency reform modeled on the UK, France, Germany, and
Norway — relevant as a policy model, not as a U.S. data point.
