# Overview: closing the US gender pay gap

**The challenge:** How might we empower girls to feel confident, supported,
and able to lead in their community? This team's angle: contribute to gender
equality (SDG 5) by working on the US gender pay gap — the economic side of
the problem, not the confidence/leadership side.

## What the evidence says so far

The size of the US gender pay gap depends entirely on which workers and which
year you count. [AAUW](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md) puts
full-time workers at **81 cents** on the dollar; [Payscale's 2026
report](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md) puts the
uncontrolled gap at **82 cents**; [National Partnership for Women &
Families](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md) counts *all*
workers, including part-time, and gets **76 cents**. These sources disagree
because they measure different populations — see
[US pay gap: the numbers](us-pay-gap-numbers.md) for all three side by side.

The gap is far worse for women of color. Payscale's uncontrolled figures:
Black women **78 cents**, Hispanic women **79 cents**, American Indian/Alaska
Native women **74 cents**, relative to white men. National Partnership's are
starker still: Black women **63 cents**, Latina women **54 cents**, Native
American women **53 cents**, relative to white non-Hispanic men. The two
sources disagree on the exact numbers but agree on the pattern: white men >
white women > women of color, with Latina and Native American women worst
off in both. See [Who the gap hits hardest](who-it-hits-hardest.md).

The gap doesn't disappear even after controlling for job title — Payscale's
controlled figure is 99 cents, not 100. One source names the structural
reasons for the remaining gap: occupational segregation, part-time work
driven by caregiving, and workplace inflexibility (insufficient paid leave
and childcare). [AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md)
See [Root causes](root-causes.md).

Two solutions already exist and are running today: AAUW's
[Work Smart](../sources/aauw-work-smart-program.md) program, which has
trained 190,000+ women in salary negotiation, and a state-by-state
[pay transparency](../sources/pay-transparency-laws-2026.md) law requiring
salary ranges in job postings, active in 14 states as of 2026. The federal
fix that would apply everywhere, the
[Paycheck Fairness Act](../sources/aauw-paycheck-fairness-act.md), has been
introduced repeatedly since at least 2019 and still has not passed. See
[Existing solutions](existing-solutions.md).

## The gaps, plainly

1. **No single number is "the" US pay gap.** Every figure in this wiki
   depends on whether it counts full-time-only or all workers, and whether
   it's controlled for job title — see
   [US pay gap: the numbers](us-pay-gap-numbers.md) before quoting any one
   figure as *the* gap.
2. **The UN's own SDG 5 target on economic resources (5.a) doesn't measure
   wages at all** — it's about land, property, and inheritance rights.
   [UN SDG 5, Target 5.a](../sources/un-sdg5-target-5a-economic-resources.md)
   See [Where this fits SDG 5](sdg5-fit.md).
3. **Causes are named but not detailed.** [Root causes](root-causes.md)
   names occupational segregation, part-time/caregiving work, and workplace
   inflexibility, but no source in this wiki yet explains any one of these in
   depth (e.g., exactly how much of the gap each cause accounts for), and the
   five "proposed solutions" named alongside them have no program or bill
   behind them yet.
