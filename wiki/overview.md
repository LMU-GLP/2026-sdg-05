# Overview: empowering girls to lead

**The challenge:** How might we empower girls to feel confident, supported,
and able to lead in their community?

## What the evidence says so far

The only dataset in this wiki that measures confidence directly — [The
Girls' Index](girls-confidence-data.md) — is national U.S. data, not
regional: self-reported confidence among 17,500+ American girls fell from
68% to 55% between 2017 and 2023, with the sharpest drop in 5th grade (86% →
68%) and the lowest point in 9th grade (50%).
[ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md)

The UN's own framework for this goal (SDG 5) targets leadership
participation (Target 5.5) and ending discrimination (Target 5.1) as the
mechanisms for empowerment, tracked globally through indicators like women's
share of parliamentary seats (27.4% as of January 2026).
[UN SDG 5 targets](../sources/un-sdg5-targets.md)

Two organizations in this wiki are already acting on pieces of this
challenge, both in Los Angeles: [Alliance for Girls](existing-programs.md)
(youth-led research) and [Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](existing-programs.md)
(a mentoring program serving 2,600+ girls a year).
[Alliance for Girls](../sources/la-girls-research.md),
[Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md)

South Korea's one source in this wiki, a **29.3%** gender pay gap — the
worst in the OECD — describes a different population (working-age women)
and a different measurement (pay) entirely.
[Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md) See
[Comparison: LA vs. South Korea](comparison-la-vs-south-korea.md) for why
that figure can't be read against the confidence data above.

## The gaps, plainly

1. **No confidence or leadership statistic exists for LA or Korea
   specifically** in this wiki. The 68%-to-55% figure is national; the 29.3%
   figure is about pay, not confidence or leadership.
2. **Korea has no youth- or girls-specific source at all yet** — no research
   organization, no program, no data on confidence or leadership.
3. **LA has organizations and a program, but not a local number.**
