# US pay gap: the numbers

Five sources in this wiki each state a different overall figure. None of
them is wrong — they're measuring different things, and two of them are even
from the same organization a year apart.

| Source | Population measured | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| [AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md) | Full-time workers | **81 cents** per dollar |
| [AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md) | Part-time workers | **76 cents** per dollar |
| [AAUW, causes report (2025)](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md) | Full-time, year-round workers | **83 cents** per dollar |
| [AAUW, causes report (2025)](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md) | All workers, including part-time/seasonal | **75 cents** per dollar |
| [Payscale 2026](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md) | All workers, uncontrolled | **82 cents** per dollar (women earn 18% less) |
| [Payscale 2026](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md) | All workers, controlled for job title/qualifications | **99 cents** per dollar |
| [National Partnership](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md) | All workers (2024 data) | **76 cents** per dollar, a **$14,640**/year difference |
| [BLS, Q2 2026](../sources/bls-womens-earnings-2026.md) | Median weekly earnings, official government data | **82.0%** ($1,131 vs. $1,380) — lines up closely with Payscale's uncontrolled figure |

**Even AAUW disagrees with itself.** Its "Simple Truth" report (undated
publication, read 2026-08-01) puts full-time workers at 81 cents; its own
March 2025 causes report puts full-time, year-round workers at 83 cents.
Same organization, same country, different year and possibly different
underlying government data release — another reason to always name which
source a figure comes from, not just say "the gap is X cents."
[AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md),
[AAUW, causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md)

**Controlled vs. uncontrolled matters.** Payscale's own data shows the
uncontrolled gap (82 cents) is far wider than the controlled gap (99 cents,
same job title and qualifications). That means most of the raw 18-cent
difference comes from *which* jobs and levels women hold, not unequal pay for
identical work — though a real, if much smaller, unexplained gap remains
even after controlling.
[Payscale 2026](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md)

**The gap widens with age and seniority.** Payscale's uncontrolled figures by
age: 20–29 is **86 cents**, 30–44 is **80 cents**, 45+ is **71 cents**. At the
executive level it drops to **69 cents** uncontrolled (92 cents controlled).
[Payscale 2026](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md)

**Lifetime cost.** AAUW's *Simple Truth* report puts the 40-year career cost
of the full-time gap at **$542,800**; National Partnership puts the national
annual cost at **almost $1.9 trillion**; AAUW's separate 2025 causes report
puts the cumulative national cost at **$61 trillion in wages lost from 1967
to 2021**. These are three different sources with three different scopes
(individual career vs. one year nationally vs. 54 years nationally) —
don't add or average them.
[AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md),
[National Partnership](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md),
[AAUW, causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md)

**Headcount.** None of the above sources gives an actual number of people —
only percentages and dollar figures. BLS is the only source in this wiki
with a headcount: **59.36 million** women employed full-time as of June
2026. [BLS](../sources/bls-womens-earnings-2026.md)
