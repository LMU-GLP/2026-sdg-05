# Where this fits SDG 5

The UN's SDG 5 target closest to economic equality is **Target 5.a**:
"Undertake reforms to give women equal rights to economic resources, as well
as access to ownership and control over land and other forms of property,
financial services, inheritance and natural resources, in accordance with
national laws."
[UN SDG 5, Target 5.a](../sources/un-sdg5-target-5a-economic-resources.md)

**Important mismatch:** Target 5.a's own indicators (5.a.1, 5.a.2) measure
land ownership and land-rights legal frameworks — not wages, not salary, not
pay. Its 2026 progress data is about agricultural land rights, joint land
registration, and inheritance law, in nearly 90% of reporting countries
having gaps there. None of it is a pay-gap statistic.
[UN SDG 5, Target 5.a](../sources/un-sdg5-target-5a-economic-resources.md)

**Target 5.1** ("End all forms of discrimination against all women and girls
everywhere") is the broader target this team's work sits under instead — its
indicator (5.1.1) tracks legal frameworks for equality generally, and its
2026 figure is that women globally hold only **64%** of the legal rights men
have. [UN SDG 5, Target 5.1](../sources/un-sdg5-target-5a-economic-resources.md)

**What this means:** this team's US pay-gap work contributes to SDG 5's
broader aim of economic gender equality, but it should not be cited as
directly measuring Target 5.a — that target is about land and property, a
different economic dimension than wages. If a pitch needs a UN target to
point at, 5.1 (discrimination broadly) is the more honest fit than 5.a.
