# Problem statement

In the United States, women working full-time earned between **81 and 83
cents** per dollar earned by men in 2024–2026, depending on the source:
AAUW's *Simple Truth* report puts it at **81 cents**
[AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md), AAUW's own
2025 causes report puts it at **83 cents**
[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md), and Payscale's
2026 uncontrolled figure is **82 cents**
[Payscale 2026](../sources/payscale-2026-gender-pay-gap-report.md) — which
lines up closely with BLS's official Q2 2026 ratio of **82.0%**
[BLS](../sources/bls-womens-earnings-2026.md). Counting all workers,
including part-time, the figure drops to **75–76 cents**
[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md),
[National Partnership](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md).

This affects at least **59.36 million women** employed full-time in the US
as of June 2026 [BLS](../sources/bls-womens-earnings-2026.md), and costs
women an estimated **$1.9 trillion a year** nationally
[National Partnership](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md), or
**$542,800** over an individual 40-year full-time career
[AAUW, Simple Truth](../sources/aauw-simple-truth-pay-gap.md).

The gap is not evenly distributed: women of color face a substantially worse
gap than white women in every source that compares against a white-men
baseline — for example, National Partnership's figures: Latina women **54
cents**, Native American women **53 cents**, Black women **63 cents**, vs.
white women's **73 cents** [National Partnership](../sources/national-partnership-wage-gap-2026.md).
Sources disagree sharply on the exact size of this disparity (see
[Who the gap hits hardest](who-it-hits-hardest.md)) and should not be
averaged.

Three structural causes are named in this wiki's evidence:
**occupational segregation** (women concentrated in lower-paying fields;
the US Department of Labor's own Women's Bureau calls this "the single
largest cause of wage gaps")
[DOL Women's Bureau](../sources/dol-womens-bureau-occupational-segregation.md),
**caregiving/the motherhood penalty** (women twice as likely as men to work
part-time, largely due to caregiving)
[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md), and
**workplace inflexibility** (insufficient paid leave and childcare access)
[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md).

**The gap this points to:** the solutions currently running — AAUW's Work
Smart negotiation training (190,000+ women reached)
[AAUW Work Smart](../sources/aauw-work-smart-program.md) and state
pay-transparency laws (14 of 50 states)
[Pay transparency laws](../sources/pay-transparency-laws-2026.md) — target
*pay-setting practices and negotiation*, not these three structural causes
directly. The measures that would actually address caregiving and workplace
inflexibility (expanded childcare, paid leave) are still a state-by-state
patchwork three decades after FMLA (1993) made leave unpaid and
job-protected only
[DOL: FMLA](../sources/dol-fmla-basics.md),
[FMLA coverage gaps](../sources/fmla-1993-coverage-gaps.md),
[State paid leave laws](../sources/bipartisan-policy-state-paid-leave-2026.md).
Occupational segregation has no solution — running or proposed — that
directly addresses it anywhere in the current evidence. See
[Policy report: complementary solutions](policy-report.md) for what each
cause's policy landscape does and doesn't already cover.
