# Problem statement

**Region:** United States, broadly (not Los Angeles or South Korea
specifically — see [Comparison: LA vs. South Korea](comparison-la-vs-south-korea.md)
for why neither region has a matching local figure).

**Who:** Girls in 9th grade. Our one confidence dataset shows this is the
single lowest point of self-reported confidence of any surveyed grade.
[ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md)

**How many:** No source in this wiki states a total U.S. population of
9th-grade girls, or how many are affected by this specific problem. The one
number we have is the survey's own scope: **more than 17,500 girls in 5th
through 12th grades** were surveyed by the Girls' Index — that describes the
sample, not the size of the affected population.
[ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md) **This is
a gap, not a figure to guess at.**

## What the evidence says

Self-reported confidence among American girls fell from **68% in 2017 to
55% in 2023.** Within that decline, **9th grade girls hit the lowest point
of any grade: 50% in 2023, down from 60% in 2017** — a steeper and lower
drop than 12th grade, which held flat at 62% across the same years.
[ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md)

A second, related figure: **women represent only 35% of the STEM workforce
despite earning half of science and engineering degrees.**
[Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md) This is a
**different measurement** from the confidence figures above — it describes
adult women's workforce representation, not girls' self-reported confidence
— so it should be read as parallel evidence of the same broad pattern (girls'
capability not converting into leadership or opportunity), not averaged or
combined with the confidence numbers into a single statistic.

## Who's already responding — and where the response is not

**Los Angeles** already has organizations acting on pieces of this problem:
[Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md) runs a
mentoring program serving **2,600+ girls annually** across 12 LAUSD campuses
in South LA, Watts, and Venice, and cites the same national 68%-to-55%
decline as its own rationale. [Alliance for Girls](../sources/la-girls-research.md)
runs youth-participatory research in LA County. **Both are local responses
to a national problem** — they demonstrate the model, but their reach (a
few thousand girls in a few LA neighborhoods) is not evidence of national
coverage, and this wiki has no source describing an equivalent response
anywhere else in the country, including no source at all on South Korea's
girls or youth (only Korea's adult gender pay gap —
[Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md) — which is excluded from
this problem statement as a different measurement in a region we are not
committing to).

## The statement

Nationally, girls' self-reported confidence has been falling for years, with
9th grade — a single grade, not the whole span surveyed — the lowest point
of any age group: only half of 9th-grade girls describe themselves as
confident, a drop from 60% just years earlier
([ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md)). We
don't have a figure for how many 9th-grade girls that represents; only the
Girls' Index survey's own scope, 17,500+ girls across grades 5–12, is
sourced ([ROX Girls' Index](../sources/rox-girls-index-confidence-data.md)).
This tracks alongside a labor-market pattern in which women remain just 35%
of the STEM workforce despite earning half of the underlying degrees
([Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md)) — a
different measurement of a related pattern, not the same statistic. At
least one model already exists at local scale: Girls Inc. of Los Angeles
reaches 2,600+ girls a year across 12 LAUSD campuses using mentoring and
research-based curricula built around exactly this confidence problem
([Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md)), alongside
Alliance for Girls' youth-led research model in the same county
([Alliance for Girls](../sources/la-girls-research.md)). Nothing in our
sources shows that model, or an equivalent, operating at national scale, or
anywhere outside Los Angeles.
