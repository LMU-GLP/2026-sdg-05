# Root causes: why the gap isn't zero

Payscale's controlled figures (same job title, same qualifications) sit at
99 cents on the dollar overall — not equal, but far closer than the
uncontrolled 82 cents. [US pay gap: the numbers](us-pay-gap-numbers.md) That
gap between controlled and uncontrolled is mostly explained by *which* jobs,
hours, and levels women hold — and one source in this wiki names the
structural reasons why.

## Named causes (AAUW, 2025)

- **Occupational segregation:** "gender, race, and occupational segregation
  continue to undermine women's earnings."
- **Part-time work disparities:** "Women were twice as likely as men to work
  part-time in 2023," driven substantially by **caregiving responsibilities**.
- **Workplace inflexibility and lack of support systems:** insufficient paid
  leave, affordable childcare, and workplace flexibility.

[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md)

## Proposed solutions (named, not detailed, not yet implemented)

The same report names five policy directions without detailing how any of
them would work: strengthening pay equity laws, reducing barriers to college
degrees, expanding childcare access, implementing paid leave, and protecting
reproductive health access.
[AAUW causes report](../sources/aauw-causes-report-2025.md) None of these
five has a program or bill behind it in this wiki yet — contrast with
[Existing solutions](existing-solutions.md), where Work Smart, state
transparency laws, and the Paycheck Fairness Act are actual running or
proposed mechanisms, not just named directions.

## A fourth set of numbers that disagrees

This source states its own overall figures — full-time **83 cents**, all
workers **75 cents** — which differ again from every other figure already in
this wiki, including AAUW's *own* "Simple Truth" report (81 cents full-time,
76 cents part-time). Its race breakdown also differs from the other two
sources with race data:

| Group | AAUW causes report (2025) | Payscale 2026 (uncontrolled) | National Partnership (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black women, full-time | 66 cents | 78 cents | — |
| Black women, all workers | 64 cents | — | 63 cents |
| Latina/Native American women, full-time | 58 cents | — | — |
| Latina women, all workers | 51 cents | — | 54 cents |

Don't average these — see [Who the gap hits hardest](who-it-hits-hardest.md)
for the full picture and why these disagree.
