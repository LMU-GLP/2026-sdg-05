# Comparing Los Angeles and South Korea

This team hasn't picked a region yet. Here's what the current sources
actually let us compare — and what they don't.

## The evidence is not the same shape in each region

| | Los Angeles | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| What's measured | No hard figure on girls' confidence or leadership; landscape/methods research and one running program's self-reported outcomes | A national pay gap among working-age women |
| Hard figure | None specific to LA | **29.3%** pay gap (OECD, 2023) [Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md) |
| Who's already working on it | [Alliance for Girls](../sources/la-girls-research.md) (research), [Girls Inc. of LA](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md) (program) | No source found |
| Population studied | Girls and gender-expansive youth | Working-age women (pay, not girls specifically) |

**Per this wiki's rules, these figures cannot be averaged or directly
compared:** Korea's 29.3% is a pay gap between adult men and women in the
labor market; nothing in the LA sources measures pay, and nothing in the
Korea source measures girls' confidence, leadership, or youth outcomes.
They are different measurements of different populations, not two numbers on
the same scale.

## What each region actually has

**Los Angeles** has organizations already doing the work — a
youth-participatory research method (Alliance for Girls) and a running
mentoring program at scale (Girls Inc., 2,600+ girls/year across 12 LAUSD
campuses) — but no hard local statistic on the confidence-and-leadership
problem itself. [Alliance for Girls](../sources/la-girls-research.md),
[Girls Inc. of Los Angeles](../sources/girls-inc-los-angeles.md)

**South Korea** has one strong, specific, well-sourced statistic (a pay gap
that is the worst in the OECD, unchanged in rank since 1996) but, in this
wiki's current sources, no research organization or program addressing girls'
confidence or leadership, and no youth-specific data at all.
[Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md)

## What this means for choosing

Choosing LA means choosing a region with people and infrastructure already in
motion, but where this team would need to find or generate its own local
number before it can defend a problem statement with a figure in it. Choosing
Korea means choosing a region with a defensible, striking statistic already
in hand, but starting from zero on who — if anyone — is already working on
girls' confidence and leadership there, and with no youth-specific evidence
yet at all.
