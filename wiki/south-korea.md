# South Korea

## The evidence: a pay gap, not a confidence measure

Korea's gender pay gap is **29.3%** (most recent OECD data, 2023) — **the
widest in the OECD**, and Korea has ranked first in this measure among OECD
members every year since joining in 1996. The OECD average is **11.3%**.
Concretely: **for every 1 million won ($725.32) earned by a man, a woman
earns 707,000 won.** [Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md)

## Why the gap persists, per this source

Korea currently discloses only headcount and average pay by gender — not pay
broken down by job type, rank, role, years of service, or employment status,
which is what would be needed to show *why* the gap exists. A proposed
amendment would require this level of disclosure at workplaces with 300+
employees. The UK and France already publish company-level data; Germany
lets employees request pay-gap information; Norway requires annual pay
audits. [Korea Times](../sources/korea-gender-pay-gap.md)

## What this wiki does not have on Korea

This wiki has exactly one Korea source, and it is about **working women's
pay**, not girls, not confidence, and not leadership programs. There is no
source here on girls specifically in Korea, no youth-confidence data for
Korea, and no program or organization working on this challenge in Korea.
That is a real gap in this team's research, not evidence that nothing exists.
