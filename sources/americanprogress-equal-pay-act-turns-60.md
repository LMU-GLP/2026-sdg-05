# Center for American Progress: What To Know About the Gender Wage Gap as the Equal Pay Act Turns 60

**URL:** https://www.americanprogress.org/article/what-to-know-about-the-gender-wage-gap-as-the-equal-pay-act-turns-60/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- **1963** (year the Equal Pay Act was signed): "the typical woman working full
  time, year-round earned just 59 cents for every dollar earned by their male
  counterparts." Counting all women workers regardless of hours: **37 cents**
  on the dollar.
- **2021:** "full-time, year-round working women typically earned 84 cents for
  every dollar earned by their male counterparts." Counting all women workers:
  **77 cents** on the dollar.
- **2021 annual gap:** full-time, year-round women "typically earned $9,954
  less than their male counterparts."
- **Cumulative:** "working women have cumulatively lost $61 trillion in wages"
  since 1967.
- **Projection at current rate of progress:** median full-time working women
  won't reach pay parity until **2056**; all women workers until **2052**.
- Women of color, full-time, 2021: Latinas **57 cents**, Black women
  **67 cents** per dollar (same full-time-men baseline as above).

## Summary

This is the clearest single-source before/after picture of the Equal Pay Act's
impact: the raw full-time gap narrowed from 59 cents in 1963 to 84 cents in
2021 — real progress over six decades, but at a pace this source projects
won't reach parity until the 2050s. This is a different year/baseline than the
AAUW and Payscale figures already in this wiki (81–83 cents, various years),
which is expected — see [US pay gap: the numbers](../wiki/us-pay-gap-numbers.md).
Don't merge this 1963→2021 trend line with the single-year snapshots from
other sources.
