# SDG 5: Gender Equality — official UN targets

- **Source:** United Nations, Department of Economic and Social Affairs, Sustainable Development Goals
- **URL:** https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal5
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## The goal

> "Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls"

## Targets that bear directly on this team's challenge

- **Target 5.1** — End all forms of discrimination against all women and girls
  everywhere. (Indicator 5.1.1: whether legal frameworks exist to promote,
  enforce, and monitor equality and non-discrimination.)
- **Target 5.5** — Ensure women's full and effective participation and equal
  opportunities for leadership at all levels of decision-making in political,
  economic, and public life. (Indicator 5.5.1: proportion of seats held by
  women in national parliaments and local governments. Indicator 5.5.2:
  proportion of women in managerial positions.)
- **Target 5.4** — Recognize and value unpaid care and domestic work.
  (Indicator 5.4.1: proportion of time spent on unpaid domestic and care work,
  by sex.)
- **Target 5.b** — Enhance the use of enabling technology, in particular
  information and communications technology, to promote the empowerment of
  women.
- **Target 5.c** — Adopt and strengthen sound policies and enforceable
  legislation for the promotion of gender equality and the empowerment of all
  women and girls at all levels.

(Full list also includes Target 5.2 on violence, Target 5.3 on child marriage
and FGM, Target 5.6 on reproductive health, and Target 5.a on economic and
land rights — see the URL above for the complete set.)

## Key figures stated on the page

- **"Nearly 1 in 3 women – estimated 840 million globally – have experienced
  physical and/or sexual violence."**
- **"Worldwide, nearly one in five girls (18%) marry before the age of 18."**
- **"230 million girls and women alive today have undergone"** female genital
  mutilation.
- Women's parliamentary representation reached **"27.4%"** as of January 2026.
- Women spend **"2.5 times"** more hours on unpaid care work than men.
- Women are **"10% less likely to own a mobile phone than men"** (2025 data).

## Summary

The UN's own framework for this challenge centers on ending discrimination
(5.1) and getting women and girls into leadership and decision-making roles
(5.5) — the same "confident, supported, and able to lead" language in this
team's brief. Its headline global figures are about violence, child marriage,
and unpaid care work, none of which is specific to Los Angeles or South Korea,
so they set the global baseline this team's regional evidence should be
measured against, not a number to import directly into either region's case.
