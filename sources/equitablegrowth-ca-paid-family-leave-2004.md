# Washington Center for Equitable Growth: New research shows paid leave increases mothers' labor force participation following childbirth

**URL:** https://equitablegrowth.org/new-research-shows-paid-leave-increases-mothers-labor-force-participation-following-childbirth/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key facts

- California's Paid Family Leave (PFL) program took effect in **2004**, the
  first paid family leave program in the US.
- Effect on new mothers: "mothers with access to paid leave in the state
  demonstrate an approximately 20 percent increase in [labor force
  participation] probability during the year of their child's birth."
- This effect "remains significant up to 5 years later, suggesting that the
  availability of state-provided paid family and medical leave can increase
  labor force participation well beyond the period of initial leave-taking."
- **Who benefited most:** "the benefits of paid leave were more pronounced and
  longer lasting for white, highly educated women in California and New
  Jersey's labor market." Lower-income families showed minimal gains,
  plausibly because California's original wage-replacement rate was only
  **55 percent** of pay.
- California has since moved to "a more progressive wage-replacement
  structure, providing low-income workers with 70 percent of their income
  while on leave."

## Summary

Paid (not just job-protected) leave measurably keeps mothers in the workforce
— a real, positive effect distinct from the unpaid federal FMLA. But the
benefit was uneven: it worked best for white, highly educated women, and
barely helped lower-income families under the original 55%-replacement design,
which is itself evidence for *how* a leave program is designed determining who
it actually reaches.
