# EEOC: Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009

**URL:** https://www.eeoc.gov/lilly-ledbetter-fair-pay-act-2009
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key facts

- Signed into law **January 29, 2009**; the first bill President Obama signed.
- Directly responds to the Supreme Court's *Ledbetter v. Goodyear Tire & Rubber
  Co.*, 550 U.S. 618 (2007), which the EEOC page describes as a ruling that
  "significantly impairs statutory protections against discrimination in
  compensation."
- The Act states: "A discriminatory compensation decision or other
  practice...occurs each time compensation is paid pursuant to the
  discriminatory compensation decision."
- It allows recovery of "back pay for up to two years preceding the filing of
  the charge" where a new discriminatory paycheck falls within the filing
  period, even if the original discriminatory pay decision happened earlier.
- The Act applies "as if enacted on May 28, 2007" — retroactive to just before
  the Ledbetter decision — and covers pay discrimination claims on the basis
  of sex, race, national origin, age, religion, and disability, not gender
  alone.

## Summary

The Ledbetter Act doesn't ban pay discrimination — the Equal Pay Act of 1963
and Title VII already did that. What it fixes is procedural: before 2009, a
worker had only 180 days from the *original* discriminatory pay decision to
sue, even if they didn't discover the underpayment until years later. The Act
resets that clock with every discriminatory paycheck, making the existing
1963/1964 protections actually usable in practice.
