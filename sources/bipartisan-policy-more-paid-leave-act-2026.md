# More Paid Leave for More Americans Act, 2026 (Bipartisan Policy Center)

**URL:** https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/whats-in-the-2026-bill-the-more-paid-leave-for-more-americans-act/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01
**Published:** 2026-07-16

## Key facts

- Introduced in the Senate on **2026-07-16** by Sens. John Boozman (R-AR) and
  Kirsten Gillibrand (D-NY); companion to a House bill introduced April 2025
  by the Bipartisan Paid Family Leave Working Group (Reps. Stephanie Bice,
  R-OK, and Chrissy Houlahan, D-PA).
- Purpose: to "incentivize states to expand access and improve coordination
  of paid family leave benefits for workers" — it does not create a federal
  paid-leave entitlement itself.
- **Title I:** competitive grants ($1.5M–$7M) to states starting new paid
  leave programs via public-private partnerships, requiring at least six
  weeks of leave aligned with FMLA-qualifying reasons.
- **Title II:** the Interstate Paid Leave Action Network (I-PLAN), a
  voluntary multi-state collaboration with grants ($1.5M–$8M/year) to
  harmonize paid-leave program definitions and administration across states.
- As of this source, the bill has only been introduced — no vote or passage
  recorded.

## Summary

Unlike the state laws in
[state-paid-leave-2026.md](bipartisan-policy-state-paid-leave-2026.md), this
is a 2026 federal bill that funds and coordinates *state* programs rather
than mandating leave nationally — it's a bridge between the patchwork and a
federal entitlement, not a replacement for either, and as of this source it
has only just been introduced.
