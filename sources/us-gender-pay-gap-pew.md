# The U.S. gender pay gap, and why it persists

- **Source:** Pew Research Center — "The Enduring Grip of the Gender Pay Gap"
  (March 1, 2023, full report) and "Gender pay gap in U.S. has narrowed
  slightly over 2 decades" (March 4, 2025, short read/update)
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/03/01/the-enduring-grip-of-the-gender-pay-gap/
  and https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/04/gender-pay-gap-in-us-has-narrowed-slightly-over-2-decades/
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

**Two different Pew figures, two different measurements — do not average them:**

- The 2023 report: **"American women typically earned 82 cents for every
  dollar earned by men"** in 2022, versus **"80 cents to the dollar"** in
  2002 — essentially flat over two decades.
- The 2025 update: **"women earned an average of 85% of what men earned"**
  in 2024 (a 15-cent gap), based on **median hourly earnings of both
  full- and part-time workers**, using Current Population Survey data from
  January 1982–December 2024 via IPUMS. This is a different, hourly-wage
  methodology from the 2022 figure above, not a one-year jump from 82 to 85.
- By age (2024, from the 2025 update): women aged 25–34 earned **"95 cents
  for every dollar earned by a man in the same age group"** — a 5-cent gap,
  much narrower than the all-worker figure.

## The three reasons this source gives for the gap

1. **Caregiving / the "motherhood penalty":** Mothers overall earn less than
   childless women, but the report finds this **"is driven mainly by
   differences in educational attainment,"** not motherhood itself once
   education is controlled for. Mothers do show reduced work hours — younger
   mothers work **"a shorter workweek – by two hours per week, on
   average."** Fathers see the opposite: a **"fatherhood wage premium"** —
   "fathers earn more than other workers... regardless of education level."
2. **Occupational segregation (lower-paying fields):** Women have gained
   ground in "social, arts and media occupations," but remain
   **"underrepresented in managerial and STEM occupations"** and
   **"overrepresented in education, health care, and personal care and
   services occupations"** — fields that pay less.
3. **Discrimination / unconscious bias:** Researchers **"have uncovered new
   evidence of hiring discrimination,"** and the report names **"gender
   stereotypes and discrimination"** as **"last-mile hurdles impeding
   further progress,"** while noting these factors remain **"difficult to
   quantify."**

## Summary

Pew's own data complicates a simple "motherhood causes the pay gap" story —
once education is controlled for, the mother/non-mother earnings gap
largely disappears, while fathers get a wage premium fathers don't "earn"
in the same sense. Occupational segregation and discrimination are named as
real contributors, but Pew is explicit that discrimination specifically is
hard to measure, which matters for any policy pitch that claims to fix it
directly.
