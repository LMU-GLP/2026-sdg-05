# Korea's gender pay gap is the world's worst. Will transparency fix it?

- **Source:** Lee Yeon-woo, *The Korea Times*, June 19, 2025
- **URL:** https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/others/20250619/koreas-gender-pay-gap-is-the-worlds-worst-will-transparency-fix-it
- **Read on:** 2026-07-30

## Key figures

- **Korea's gender pay gap is 29.3%** (most recent OECD data, 2023) — **the widest in the OECD.**
- Korea has ranked **first among OECD members for its gender pay gap every year since joining in 1996.**
- The **OECD average is 11.3%.**
- Concretely: **for every 1 million won ($725.32) earned by a man, a woman earns 707,000 won.**
- Australia's gap, by comparison, is about 11%.

## The measurement problem, which is the interesting part

Korea currently discloses only the number of male and female employees and
average pay per person by gender. That isn't enough to find *why* the gap
exists. A proposed amendment would require workplaces with more than 300
employees to disclose gender and employment data broken down by job type, rank,
role, years of service, and employment status. The UK and France already publish
company-level data; Germany lets employees request pay-gap information; Norway
requires annual pay audits.

> "The burden of proof is shifting: companies must now 'demonstrate' fairness,
> not just declare it."

## Summary

Korea has had the worst gender pay gap in the OECD every single year since it
joined in 1996, currently 29.3% against an OECD average of 11.3%. The obstacle
isn't awareness — Koreans already name it as the most serious workplace gender
issue — it's that the country doesn't collect data detailed enough to prove
where the gap comes from.
