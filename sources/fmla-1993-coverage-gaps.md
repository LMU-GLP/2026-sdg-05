# FMLA (1993): signing date and coverage gaps (Wikipedia)

**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_and_Medical_Leave_Act_of_1993
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key facts

- President Bill Clinton signed the FMLA into law on **February 5, 1993**.
- As of 2003, "only about 60% of private sector workers are covered" by
  FMLA due to the minimum employer-size requirement.
- Adding the minimum hours-worked requirement, "only 46% of private sector
  workers are eligible for leave."
- In 2007, of **141.7 million** US workers, **76.1 million** were eligible
  for FMLA leave — meaning roughly **65.6 million** did not meet eligibility
  criteria.
- Only **8 to 17.1 percent** of covered, eligible workers actually used FMLA
  leave in 2005.

## Summary

Even among workers technically eligible for FMLA, the law's employer-size
and hours-worked thresholds shut out roughly half of the private-sector
workforce as of the mid-2000s, and unpaid leave means many eligible workers
still don't use it — the gap [state paid leave
laws](bipartisan-policy-state-paid-leave-2026.md) and the [More Paid Leave
for More Americans Act](bipartisan-policy-more-paid-leave-act-2026.md) are
both trying to close, thirty years after FMLA passed.
