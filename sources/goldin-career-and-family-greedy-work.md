# Harvard Gazette: Excerpt from "Career and Family" by Claudia Goldin

**URL:** https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/10/excerpt-from-career-and-family-by-claudia-goldin/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key quotes

- On "greedy work": "Work, for many on the career track, is greedy. The
  individual who puts in overtime, weekend time, or evening time will earn a
  lot more."
- On specialization within couples: "couples with children or other care
  responsibilities would gain by doing a bit of specialization" — one partner
  stays flexible while the other prioritizes the greedy job's demands.
- On when the gap opens up: "It is only further along in their lives, about 10
  years after college graduation, that large differences in pay for men and
  women become apparent." These changes "typically begin a year or two after a
  child is born and almost always negatively impact women's careers."
- On why equal-pay law alone hasn't closed the gap: "the lion's share of the
  pay gap comes from something else" beyond direct pay discrimination or
  occupational segregation — and "the gender pay gap among college graduates
  has remained flat in the last several decades, despite improvements in
  women's credentials and positions."

## Summary

Goldin (2023 Nobel laureate in economics) locates the *remaining* gap — the
part that isn't explained by which jobs women hold — in "greedy work": jobs
that pay disproportionately more per hour for long, inflexible hours. Because
couples with children still specialize, and mothers still take on the
flexible role, the parenthood-linked pay gap persists even among equally
credentialed college graduates, which is why laws banning direct
discrimination (Equal Pay Act, Ledbetter Act) don't reach it.
