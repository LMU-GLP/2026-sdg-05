# State Paid Family Leave Laws Across the U.S. (Bipartisan Policy Center)

**URL:** https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/state-paid-family-leave-laws-across-the-u-s/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01
**Updated:** 2026-04-23

## Key figures

- **24 states plus the District of Columbia** have paid family and medical
  leave laws: **14 mandatory** programs (California, Connecticut,
  Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island, Washington, Colorado, Delaware,
  Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Oregon, Virginia, New York, plus DC) and
  **10 voluntary** programs (Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Kentucky, New
  Hampshire, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont).
- Quote: "Fourteen states and the District of Columbia have established
  comprehensive, mandatory state paid family leave systems."
- California started the trend, enacted in 2002, effective 2004.
- Virginia is the most recent state to enact a mandatory program (2026,
  effective 2028/2029).
- Of the 24 programs, 22 have been implemented; the rest are not yet active.

## Summary

Paid family leave in the US is a 20-plus-year-old state-by-state patchwork,
not a federal program — 14 states plus DC mandate it, but roughly
three-quarters of states either have no law or only a voluntary one, meaning
most US women have no legal right to paid leave tied to caregiving.
