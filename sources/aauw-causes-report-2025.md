# AAUW: Gender Pay AAUW's New Report Exposes the Deep-Rooted Causes of Pay Inequity (Equal Pay Day 2025)

**URL:** https://www.aauw.org/resources/news/media/press-releases/gender-pay-aauws-new-report-exposes-the-deep-rooted-causes-of-pay-inequity-on-equal-pay-day-2025/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01
**Published:** 2025-03-25

## Key figures

- Full-time, year-round workers: women earn **83 cents** per dollar earned by men.
- Including part-time and seasonal workers: **75 cents** per dollar.
- Latina and Native American women, full-time: **58 cents** per dollar vs. white, non-Hispanic men.
- Black women, full-time: **66 cents** per dollar.
- Latina women, including part-time: **51 cents** per dollar.
- Black women, including part-time: **64 cents** per dollar.
- Cumulative: US women lost an estimated **$61 trillion in wages** from 1967 to 2021.

## Named causes

- **Occupational segregation:** "gender, race, and occupational segregation continue to undermine women's earnings."
- **Part-time work disparities:** "Women were twice as likely as men to work part-time in 2023."
- **Caregiving responsibilities** driving that part-time work.
- **Workplace inflexibility and lack of support systems:** insufficient paid leave, affordable childcare, and workplace flexibility.

## Proposed solutions (named, not detailed)

Strengthening pay equity laws, reducing barriers to college degrees, expanding childcare access, implementing paid leave, protecting reproductive health access.

## Summary

This report is the first source in this wiki to name specific structural
causes of the pay gap — occupational segregation, part-time work driven by
caregiving, and lack of workplace flexibility — rather than just the size of
the gap. Its own figures (83 cents full-time, 58 cents for Latina/Native
American women) differ again from every other pay-gap figure already in this
wiki, reinforcing that no single number is "the" gap.
