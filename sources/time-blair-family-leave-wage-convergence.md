# TIME: Why the U.S. Gender Wage Gap Hasn't Narrowed in Decades (summarizing Peter Q. Blair's NBER research)

**URL:** https://time.com/6284420/family-leave-policy-us-gender-wage-gap/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures

- Central finding: "family-leave policies caused a 76% slowdown in the rate at
  which the gender wage gap was closing."
- Mechanism cited — statistical discrimination: "Firms, engaging in a practice
  that economists refer to as statistical discrimination, may take the
  expected difference into consideration during the hiring or promotion
  process."
- Behavioral asymmetry driving that discrimination: "Women take approximately
  four times more days of leave, compared to men," following a birth or
  adoption.
- Earnings ten years after a leave policy takes effect: a "white man...has
  annual earnings which were almost $3,000 higher than expected," while "white
  women's annual earnings had declined by $197 following the policy."

## Summary

This is the counter-evidence to the FMLA-is-good-for-women story: because
women take far more of the available leave days than men, employers respond
by statistically discriminating against women in hiring and promotion,
slowing wage convergence rather than accelerating it. The mechanism (unequal
leave-taking → employer discrimination) is the load-bearing finding here, not
just the existence of leave law — see
[Institute for Family Studies — parental leave and wage convergence](ifstudies-parental-leave-wage-convergence.md)
for a second source giving different but directionally consistent numbers.
