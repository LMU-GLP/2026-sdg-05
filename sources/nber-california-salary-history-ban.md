# NBER Working Paper 27054: Information and the Persistence of the Gender Wage Gap — Early Evidence from California's Salary History Ban

**URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w27054
**Date read:** 2026-08-01

## Key figures (from abstract)

- Studies California's salary history ban (SHB) using "both difference-in-
  difference and synthetic control approaches."
- Headline finding: "the gender earnings ratio increased by 1 percent in
  states with SHBs."
- The gain was concentrated: "principally driven by those who have recently
  switched jobs," and largest among "households with all children over 5
  years old, by workers over 35."

## Summary

A more conservative, single-state estimate than the Boston University review
in [HR Dive](hrdive-salary-history-ban-2025.md): a 1-percentage-point rise in
the gender earnings ratio, concentrated among job-switchers and workers whose
children are already school-age. Both sources agree on direction (bans help,
modestly, and mainly for people changing jobs) but not on size — keep both
rather than averaging them, consistent with how this wiki already treats
disagreeing pay-gap figures.
