# HR Dive: Salary history bans shrink gender- and race-based pay gaps, researchers find

**URL:** https://www.hrdive.com/news/salary-history-bans-shrink-gender-race-based-pay-gaps/805190/
**Date read:** 2026-08-01
**Published:** 2025-11-05 (citing a 2024 study)

## Key figures

- Boston University School of Law literature review (published 2025-11-05),
  drawing on a 2024 study of wage outcomes: "newly hired female workers in
  jurisdictions with bans saw salary increases 7.8% greater than their peers"
  in jurisdictions without bans.
- New hires overall: "an average salary increase of 7.9% in places with a ban
  versus a 3.9% average increase in those without."
- Non-white job-changers in ban jurisdictions saw "a 7.8% increase in wages as
  well as a net 5.8% increase in pay."
- **Adoption:** "Nearly half of U.S. states had implemented such laws as of
  April 2025," plus more than 20 local jurisdictions.

## Summary

Salary history bans — laws stopping employers from asking applicants what they
previously earned — are a distinct policy from the state pay-transparency
(salary-range-disclosure) laws already in this wiki. About half of US states
now have one. The cited research finds a real, measurable wage bump for women
and non-white workers who change jobs under a ban, though see
[NBER — California salary history ban](nber-california-salary-history-ban.md)
for a more modest effect-size estimate from a single-state study.
