The problem

Three documented causes. We're not picking one.

Caregiving & time out of the workforce

Not a simple “motherhood penalty” — once education is controlled for, the raw mother/non-mother pay gap is “driven mainly by differences in educational attainment.” What's real: younger mothers work two fewer hours per week on average, and fathers get a “fatherhood wage premium” that education alone doesn't explain.

— Pew Research

Occupational segregation

Women are underrepresented in managerial and STEM occupations and overrepresented in education, health care, and personal care/services — fields that pay less.

— Pew Research

Discrimination & unconscious bias

Researchers have “uncovered new evidence of hiring discrimination,” named as a “last-mile hurdle” — though Pew is explicit these factors remain “difficult to quantify.”

— Pew Research

The ask

One package. Three parts. Matched to the causes above.

  • Caregiving support Answers cause #1 — addresses reduced hours during caregiving years and the unmatched fatherhood wage premium.
  • Incentives into higher-paying fields Answers cause #2 — occupational segregation into lower-paying, female-dominated fields.
  • Pay transparency & disaggregated reporting requirements Answers cause #3 — discrimination that's hard to prove without better data. Mirrors a reform Korea is currently debating (not a U.S. precedent) — Korea Times.

How it works

Federal level. No lobbyists of our own.

  1. Research — translate data like Pew's into concrete policy language, one proposal per cause.
  2. Coalition-building — recruit nonprofits, unions, and companies to publicly co-sign the package, adding political weight without hiring lobbyists.
  3. Public campaign — media, social, and grassroots pressure aimed at voters and press, so coalition partners carry the direct legislative engagement.

What this isn't

Deliberately left out

  • Direct lobbying — no congressional meetings or testimony run by us.
  • State-level policy work — federal only, even as a proof-of-concept path.
  • International/comparative policy work — Korea's reform debate is background, not something we act on.
  • Direct services to women — no job training or legal aid. Policy and advocacy only.

Open gap

No source we've found measures whether caregiving support, occupational-segregation incentives, or pay transparency actually moves the U.S. pay gap. The case for this package currently rests on Pew's diagnosis of the causes — not on evidence that these specific interventions fix them.
